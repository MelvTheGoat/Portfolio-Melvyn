FPL AI Manager

Two models play the 2026/27 Fantasy Premier League season side by side — one under the real constraints, one with perfect freedom — to measure what continuity actually costs. Scored with real FPL points against the official gameweek average.

View on GitHub →

01The problem

Every FPL manager pays for continuity — one free transfer a week, −4 for each extra, and a squad in January shaped by what was bought in August — and nobody can see the bill, because you only ever get to play one of the two possible seasons. The Manager carries one squad all year under the real constraints; Best XI of the Week rebuilds the best legal squad from scratch every gameweek. They are not competing. The gap between them is the measurement.

02The result

Headline number

Both models are scored with real FPL points pulled from the API and never recalculated, tracked against the official gameweek average — the same yardstick every human manager is measured by. Nothing is graded against a simulation of itself. 574 tests, weighted toward the rules engine and the projection, where a silent error does the most damage.

03Architecture

FPL API (cached, rate-limited) → rules engine as pure logic → component-built expected points → ILP squad + lineup + captaincy optimiser (PuLP/CBC) → Best XI (one gameweek) vs The Manager (transfers, hits, chips) → locked picks, never regenerated

04The hardest decision

The no-leakage rule is enforced by the schema, not by discipline

The season was already under way, so gameweek 1 onward had to be reconstructed — and a reconstruction that peeks is worse than no reconstruction, because it still looks fine. Three structures carry the rule: prices are snapshotted per gameweek so a GW7 decision is costed at GW7 prices; projections are keyed by the deadline they were made before; and writing picks for a gameweek that already has them raises rather than overwrites. That last refusal is the load-bearing one — regenerating a past gameweek with hindsight would quietly invalidate every result after it. The one honest leak is stated outright: backfilled gameweeks read injury status as it is now, because the API does not publish its history.

05What the results actually showed

Measured, not assumed

After the first four gameweeks: the Manager on 171 points, Best XI on 196 — the 25-point gap the project exists to measure — and, less comfortably, neither model has beaten the FPL average yet (0/4 each). Small sample, but that is reported as it stands rather than explained away. Four bugs also reached the published site, and all four shared one shape worth naming: they produced plausible output instead of an error. A season published as zeros reads as a season that went badly, not as a broken deployment — which is why setup stages now gate on results before picks, and why stale-score detection re-scores whenever the results behind a score are newer than the score.

06What's next

A real backtest against a held-out prior season. The projection's component weights are currently reasoned rather than fitted, and only a held-out season says whether it is good or merely sensible.
